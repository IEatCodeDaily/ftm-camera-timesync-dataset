## Findings

1. **No retained no-timesync timing comparison was found.** The firmware exposes `mac` (local MAC clock) as an unsynchronized control, but none of the 30 retained `test-output/timing-methods/*/*/result.json` entries are a `*:mac` experiment. The GPIO2 “baseline” in the measurement document already uses FTM models and has corrupt/nonqualifying follower channels; it cannot be relabeled no-timesync. Unsynchronized camera preview elsewhere is a transport/FPS result, not inter-node clock error evidence.
2. **No simple NTP timing experiment or implementation was found in the reviewed sources.** The timing source list is `ftm`, `frozen`, `tsf`, `mac`; TSF is access-point timing and must not be called NTP. NTP performance must remain “not measured” until a real four-timestamp exchange implementation and external comparison are available.
3. **Frozen FTM is holdover after an initial FTM fit, not no synchronization.** Source code copies a valid affine model on first use and keeps using its slope and offset. Its 25-second result measures that model plus GPIO scheduling; isolated outliers do not independently establish oscillator drift. Evidence: `firmware/ftm_clocksync/main/strobe_gpio.c:268`; `test-output/timing-methods/20260908-170156/00-gpt-frozen/`.
4. **The strongest synchronization-source comparison is exploratory, same-backend GPTimer, same build, 25 seconds per source.** FTM: follower SD 0.452/0.581/0.388 us, worst 1.500 us; frozen FTM: 1.132/13.675/41.390 us, worst 211.501 us; AP TSF: 5.851/4.958/6.478 us, worst 35.125 us. Each has 25 edges on all four channels. These are single sequential trials on build `34e1a3df22c26af8`, which predates epoch repair; no randomized order, independent repeats or identical age since boot is established. They support observations in these records, not a general method ranking. Evidence: `test-output/timing-methods/20260908-170156/00-gpt-{ftm,frozen,tsf}/result.json`.
5. **Post-repair MCPWM results are credible limited-duration relative-output evidence.** Two same-build loaded trials have 60 edges per channel and follower SD ranges 0.481–0.704 us; worst absolute offsets 1.562 and 1.625 us. A responder-only restart followed by a successful 60-second trial on the subsequent build has SD 0.563–0.582 us and worst 1.937 us. They do not prove an absolute 1-us bound: observed extrema exceed 1 us. These three trials are 180 recorded seconds per node, split over two builds and a restart, not a continuous 180-second experiment. Evidence: `test-output/timing-methods/20260908-173016/{00,01}-mcpwm-ftm/` and `20260908-174159/00-mcpwm-ftm/`.
6. **Reported “load” is health polling, not full camera workload.** Those three MCPWM result JSON files record 233–239 successful requests per node per capture (nominal configured ceiling 5 Hz). Subsequent camera/FPS results cannot establish the same timing under camera load because analyzer acquisition had failed. Firmware after the timing-validated image changed repeatedly; readiness on the latest build does not transfer external timing validation automatically.
7. **Keep failed and inconclusive evidence visible.** `20260908-170133/00-task-ftm` intentionally arms only node 2; its inventory worst=0.000 is an empty-comparison artifact, not perfect synchronization. `20260908-172251` lacks D3 pulses, and the last MCPWM run includes probe reconnection; do not treat those as valid four-node repetitions. Acquisition/export errors yield no timing verdict. The long pre-repair `20260908-171431` campaign exposes approximately 475-ms error and missing edges and must be retained as a firmware failure, not pooled into repaired estimates.
8. **Small SD can coexist with huge bias.** Pre-repair GPTimer/high-priority records have few-microsecond SD and roughly 475-ms offset. Always present mean signed offset, SD, p95 absolute error, worst absolute error and matched/expected counts together. No score based on SD alone is sufficient.
9. **Two matching algorithms have different estimands.** `run_timing_methods.py` independently assigns each follower raw edge its closest reference edge within 0.5 s, permitting reuse and measuring nearest periodic phase. The diagnostic analyzer uses pulse qualification and a 0.25-s matching window; strict analyzer uses a common time window and cadence criteria. Do not merge their sample counts without explanation. Nearest-phase alignment cannot detect integer-second epoch errors; startup artifacts or duplicate edges can distort it.
10. **Measurement precision is not traceable accuracy.** 16 MS/s gives 62.5-ns sample spacing; the analyzer timebase and channel/cable delay are not calibrated. GPIO timing includes the clock model, reference-node edge, conversion, scheduler/backend and wiring. It is not UTC accuracy, isolated FTM fit accuracy or camera exposure synchrony. Three follower series share the same reference edge and are correlated; 60 serial pulse samples are not 60 independent hardware experiments. Avoid naive per-pulse significance tests or confidence intervals implying independent repetitions.
11. **Startup artifact is experimentally documented.** The static-HIGH control produced a synthetic transition at 128 us; default result counts remain raw. This can explain initial artifacts only, not later excursions. The final restart CSV begins HIGH at t=0, followed by falling edges around 41.439 ms; the raw-edge parser correctly does not count the initial HIGH as a rising edge. Evidence: `test-output/timing-static/20260908-173946/` and `test-output/timing-methods/20260908-174159/00-mcpwm-ftm/digital.csv`.

## Minimum additional experiment needed for the requested comparison

Use a common repaired firmware and identical GPIO backend for free-running local clock, simple local NTP-style four-timestamp exchange, live FTM, frozen FTM holdover and AP TSF if available. Keep reference node, clock units, pulse generation and workload fixed. Record both absolute initial epoch/phase mismatch and change from each trial's initial offset for free-running clocks; do not secretly align away the baseline's offset. Counterbalance order, collect multiple trials and extend beyond several 281.475-second FTM wraps, recording reconnect/restart outcomes separately. Include idle and real camera-load conditions. Record successful/failed acquisitions, cadence failures and integer-second identity. If analyzer recovery is unsuccessful, deliver an explicitly incomplete empirical comparison and reproducible protocol; software packet telemetry alone cannot replace external GPIO timing.
